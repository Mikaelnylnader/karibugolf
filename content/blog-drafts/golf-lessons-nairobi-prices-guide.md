Golf lessons in Nairobi range from short swing assessments to private sessions, group classes and on-course coaching. A complete beginner can start with one assessment, confirm what the quoted price includes, and choose a coach who can turn the first session into a simple practice plan.

A handicap, club membership and a full set of clubs are unnecessary before your first lesson. Confirm the venue, duration, equipment, practice balls and any facility charge in writing when you book.

When comparing quotations, ask whether the amount includes:

- Practice balls or a ball bucket.
- Range or facility access.
- Club hire.
- Launch-monitor or video analysis.
- Green fees and caddie fees for a playing lesson.
- A written practice plan or follow-up notes.

These extras can make the lowest headline price more expensive by the time you reach the range.

## Which type of golf lesson should you book?

### Swing assessment

A short assessment suits a new student or a golfer who does not know what to work on. The coach watches your current motion, identifies the biggest priority and gives you one or two drills. It is also a low-risk way to find out whether the coach's communication style works for you.

After 30 minutes, you should know which part of your game to work on first and how to practise it.

### Private lesson

A private golf lesson gives the coach time to work on one area in depth. That might be contact with irons, driver direction, pitching, bunker play or putting. It is usually the best choice if you want individual feedback and a practice plan tailored to your game.

Tell the coach what happens on the course instead of diagnosing your own swing. "My drives start right and curve farther right" gives the coach more to work with than "I need a perfect backswing."

### Lesson package

A package is worthwhile when the sessions form a progression. Ask what each lesson is expected to cover, how much practice should happen between sessions, and whether dates expire. Four focused lessons with practice between them can be more useful than four lessons booked on consecutive days.

### Group golf class

Group lessons can make golf more social and reduce the cost per person. They work well for couples, friends, juniors and corporate beginners. The trade-off is less individual coaching time, so confirm the maximum group size and whether everyone is at a similar level.

### Short-game lesson

Putting, chipping and pitching affect every round. A short-game lesson often produces quicker scoring gains than chasing more distance. It is especially useful if you can reach the green area comfortably but lose shots from inside roughly 50 metres.

### On-course playing lesson

A range swing is only part of golf. A playing lesson covers club selection, targets, uneven lies, trouble management, pace and routine. It is most useful after you can make safe contact and understand basic etiquette. Confirm green fees, caddie fees and access rules separately.

## What happens in a first golf lesson?

A good first session normally begins with a conversation. The coach should ask about your experience, goals, injuries, available practice time and equipment. After a warm-up and a few shots, the lesson should narrow to a small number of priorities.

For a complete beginner, expect some combination of:

1. Safety and where to stand.
2. Grip, posture and alignment.
3. Balanced motion and basic contact.
4. A simple putting or chipping introduction.
5. One practice drill and a plan for the next visit.

Judge the lesson by whether you understand the next action and can repeat it safely. The number of balls you hit matters less.

## How many golf lessons does a beginner need?

There is no honest universal number. One lesson can make a first practice session safer and more enjoyable. Several lessons with practice between them can build enough contact, short game and course awareness for a first round.

A sensible first-month plan is:

- Week 1: assessment and basic contact.
- Week 2: one independent practice session using the assigned drill.
- Week 3: private lesson on the biggest remaining issue, plus putting or chipping.
- Week 4: practise again and ask whether a short on-course lesson is appropriate.

Someone preparing for a corporate golf day may need a different plan from a junior building long-term technique or an established golfer trying to lower a handicap.

## How to choose a golf coach in Nairobi

Technical vocabulary matters less than a clear explanation. Look for a coach who can adapt the lesson to your body and goals, then measure whether the change works.

Ask these questions before booking:

- Do you regularly teach complete beginners or players at my level?
- Where will the lesson take place?
- What is included in the quoted KSh price?
- Can I borrow or rent clubs?
- Will I receive a drill or notes after the lesson?
- Is video or launch-monitor data used, and why?
- What is the cancellation or rain policy?
- When would you recommend an on-course lesson?

A coach should be able to explain the plan without promising an instant cure.

## Should you buy clubs before taking lessons?

Borrow or rent clubs for the first session if that option is available. A coach can help confirm whether you play right- or left-handed and whether the shaft, length, weight and grip are sensible for you before you buy.

When you are ready to buy, use the [Karibu guide to choosing your first golf clubs](/blog/first-golf-clubs-kenya/) and compare the live [golf irons](/shop/clubs/golf_irons/), [drivers](/shop/clubs/drivers/) and [putters](/shop/clubs/putters/). Ask for the exact shaft material and flex on an iron set rather than assuming every model is the same.

## Practising between lessons

Practise between coaching sessions, but keep each visit short enough to stay focused. Pick a target, use the assigned drill, and stop when fatigue changes the motion.

If a launch monitor is available, use a small number of relevant measurements. A beginner rarely needs every number on the screen. Carry distance, start direction and strike pattern may be enough to make the session useful.

Write down three things after every practice:

1. What was the goal?
2. What produced the best shots?
3. What will you repeat next time?

That note tells your coach more than "I practised a lot."

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I take golf lessons in Nairobi without membership?

Yes. Karibu Golf offers private coaching in Nairobi, and some clubs and ranges publish non-member options. Venue access varies, so identify yourself as a non-member when requesting a booking.

### What should I wear to a golf lesson?

Ask the venue. A collared or neat sports top, tailored shorts or trousers, and clean shoes with grip are safe starting choices. Bring water, sunscreen, a cap and a light rain layer.

### Are golf clubs included in a lesson?

Do not assume so. Ask whether loan or rental clubs are available and whether they cost extra.

### Is a group lesson good for a beginner?

Yes, especially when the group is small and at a similar level. Private coaching gives more individual attention; a group can be more social and economical.

### What is the best first golf lesson?

For most people, it is an assessment that covers safety, contact and one clear practice priority. You can then decide whether a private lesson, package or beginner group suits you.
