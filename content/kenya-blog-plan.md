# Karibu Golf: Kenya Blog and SEO Plan

Research date: 2 September 2026. Status: editorial plan for owner review; all three starter articles are DRAFTS.

## Executive recommendation

Start with three connected guides: learning golf in Nairobi, budgeting for golf in Kenya, and buying a first set of clubs. They answer practical questions before a purchase and can link naturally to relevant Karibu categories. Follow with narrower equipment and local-access guides, not a stream of generic golf news.

The priority order below is an editorial inference from reader intent, verified local facilities and relevance to the catalogue. It is not a ranking by keyword volume, competition score or predicted traffic. No keyword-volume dataset or Karibu Search Console data was available. Search-result presence shows an existing subject ecosystem, not proven demand.

## Evidence and boundaries

- [Golf Park](https://golfpark.or.ke/) describes pay-and-play golf and lists a range, putting green and School of Golf. Its site is relevant for a beginner starting-point guide. Do not repeat “only” or affordability superlatives as independent facts.
- [Muthaiga’s professional page](https://www.muthaigagolfclub.com/club-professional/) lists instruction, beginner material and non-member coaching, with separate charges and exclusions. This supports a booking checklist, not an assumption of unrestricted access or guaranteed lesson availability.
- [Kenya Golf Union](https://kenyagolfunion.org/) and its [WHS conversion page](https://kenyagolfunion.org/whs-conversion/) are official starting points for later club and handicap research. The current research only established these pages; a detailed handicap article needs further rule review.
- [The R&A Rule 4](https://www.randa.org/en/rog/the-rules-of-golf/rule-4) establishes the fourteen-club maximum and allows fewer. It does not tell an individual which clubs to buy.
- [The R&A junior-development article](https://www.randa.org/en/articles/kenya-sets-high-standard-for-junior-golf-development) is historical context from 2022, not evidence of current programmes or schedules.
- Search results also surfaced Kenyan equipment sellers and beginner programmes. These provide topic leads, not verified product prices, endorsement, comparative quality or market-size evidence.

## SEO and editorial implementation

Use permanent, descriptive article URLs; one clear page heading; a unique title and useful meta description; readable sections; and descriptive links to related guides and relevant shop categories. Publish original, people-first advice with named authors or an honest editorial-team identity, reviewed sources and real update dates. Avoid keyword stuffing and copied competitor text. These recommendations follow [Google’s SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide).

Add accurate BlogPosting/Article structured data matching visible content, including headline, author, publication and modification dates. Use a representative licensed article image only when one genuinely exists, not an invented experience photo. Validate markup before release. Structured data helps understanding but does not guarantee rich results: [Google’s Article documentation](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/article) and [structured-data introduction](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/intro-structured-data).

Keep drafts out of the public blog and sitemap. Link published articles from the blog index and site navigation. After the owner authorizes a public deployment, submit the real public sitemap and inspect example URLs in Search Console. A localhost blog cannot attract public search traffic. Google does not guarantee indexing or rankings; metadata alone is not an audience strategy.

Proposed cadence: one reviewed article per week, beginning with the three starters. This is a manageable editorial suggestion, not a volume requirement. After publication, use actual Search Console queries, clicks and impressions plus aggregate enquiries to decide what to improve. Do not promise traffic before data exists.
